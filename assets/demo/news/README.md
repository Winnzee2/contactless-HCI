# The Meridian — offline news demo

A realistic fictional newspaper for demonstrating Local Control in everyday browsing. Open `index.html` in Safari or another browser. It works directly from disk; no server, build, internet connection, account, or external assets are needed.

## Features

- Editorial front page with original vector illustrations and a latest-stories sidebar.
- Six full-length fictional articles across World, Technology, Science, Culture, and City.
- Section pages, related stories, and normal browser back/forward navigation.
- Instant search across titles, descriptions, sections, and article text.
- Reading-list bookmarks stored locally in the browser, with a session-only fallback if storage is unavailable.
- Adjustable article text size, responsive layouts, keyboard focus indicators, and semantic controls.
- Repeated “Read story” links for demonstrating numbered target selection.

## Suggested hands-free demonstration

1. Start Local Control, enable control, and keep your hand visible.
2. Open the newspaper. Point near the navigation, say “Technology,” then pinch thumb and middle finger to open it.
3. Say the visible article headline, then pinch to read the story.
4. Hold thumb and pinky together and move your hand vertically to scroll.
5. Return to the article controls, say “Save story,” and pinch. Open “Reading list” to see the saved article.
6. Say “last page” to demonstrate browser history.
7. Return Home and scroll until multiple “Read story” links are visible. Point near them and say “Read story.” When Local Control shows numbered choices, say a number, then pinch to open that article.
8. Open another article in a separate browser tab to demonstrate “tab left,” “tab right,” and “close tab.”

Search accepts typed input; the newspaper itself does not transcribe speech. Voice targeting and hand gestures come from Local Control using ordinary screen discovery, with no website-specific integration. Target matching depends on visible text and the app’s sensing radius.

All stories, authors, quotations, and illustrations belong to this fictional demo edition. The date is fixed; this is not a live news feed. The page never requests camera or microphone access, sends analytics, or calls a network service.
