01 / A more direct connection

From “over there”
to right there.

Voice and gestures share the work. Say the label you’re looking at, let the cursor settle, then make a deliberate pinch to act.

01 — FIND IT
“Technology”

Say what you see.

Local speech recognition turns your words into text. Screen understanding finds readable labels near your cursor. Multiple matches? Say the number of your choice.

02 — REACH IT
↗

Let your hand lead.

Your index finger moves the pointer. Adjustable movement gain and smoothing help you cross the screen; a target lock steadies the cursor for your next action.

03 — DO IT
● ↔ ●

Pinch with purpose.

Thumb + middle finger clicks. Thumb + ring finger right-clicks. Hold thumb + pinky and move vertically to scroll. Two quick left pinches give you a double-click.

A few useful details
Does this run entirely offline?

The desktop app’s control loop can run offline once its models and dependencies are installed. Initial setup and downloading source code require connectivity. Your browser still needs internet for online websites.

Will it work with every app and every target?

It works across apps using screen text and accessibility labels. Hidden controls, unreadable text, and unlabeled custom canvases can be difficult to target. Screen discovery currently focuses on the primary display. Hand tracking depends on lighting, visibility, and camera angle.

Does the website use my camera or microphone?

No. The interactive illustration above simulates the workflow. Camera and microphone use happens in the native desktop app after you grant macOS permissions.

Where can I find the full feature list?

Read the feature guide on GitHub for supported gestures, voice commands, settings, architecture, and current limits.
